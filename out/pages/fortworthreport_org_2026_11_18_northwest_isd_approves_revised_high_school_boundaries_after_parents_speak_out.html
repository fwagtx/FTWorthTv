Northwest ISD approves revised high school boundaries after parents speak out

by Matthew Sgroi November 18, 2026 5:00 pmNovember 18, 2026 4:59 pm

Northwest ISD trustees may have to redraw high school attendance boundaries as soon as next year, despite having just approved a new map.

The possibility comes after trustees agreed Nov. 9, in response to community concerns, to keep hundreds of students in their current attendance zones who otherwise would have changed schools. The decision potentially accelerates the need for another round of rezoning.

“We could be having this conversation a year from now, depending on enrollment,” Superintendent Mark Foust said.

Trustees voted 6-0 to approve revised boundaries for next school year, when Davis High School, the district’s fourth comprehensive high school, opens. Trustee Lillian Rauch was absent.

The new boundaries reflect several changes made after weeks of community feedback about longer commutes, friendships and extracurricular activities. Trustees also made one final adjustment during the meeting, keeping about 50 additional students in the Northwest High School attendance zone.

Northwest High already enrolls about 3,400 students — roughly 155 more than projected this school year. That means boundaries might have to be drawn again as soon as next year.

But the changes offered relief for some families after weeks of uncertainty. 

Jennifer Hill, a resident of Newark, a small city straddling the Tarrant and Wise county line, was one of three parents who brought more concerns to trustees on Nov. 9.

Her neighborhood, Thousand Oaks, would have moved from Northwest High’s attendance zone to Eaton High School’s. The proposal brought to the board in response to earlier concerns still would have separated her children from old friends, she said. 

“My children will now be separated from more than half of the friends they’ve grown up with since elementary school,” Hill said. “And that’s what makes the revision so much harder for us to accept.” Only a small change in the boundary line would fix the problem, she said. 

She hoped trustees would reconsider the boundaries around Oak Hills Drive and Post Oak Drive in Newark. Based on numbers residents gathered, Hill argued an adjustment would only impact seven incoming students over the next three school years.

“I’m not asking you to redraw the entire district,” Hill said. “I’m asking you to take another look at two streets, a handful of students, and a neighborhood that’s been a part of the Northwest High School community for years.”

Ultimately, trustees kept Thousand Oaks and surrounding neighborhoods zoned for Northwest High. 

The attendance boundary committee’s earlier changes to the plan had kept about 210 students at Northwest High who had faced a move to Eaton High, Sarah Stewart, Northwest ISD’s chief of facilities, told trustees. 

By the time of the board’s Nov. 9 vote, the number of students moving from Northwest to Eaton had shrunk from an estimated 407 to about 147. 

Officials also revised boundaries that affected families previously assigned to Davis High School.

Residents of Bridgeview, Spring Ranch and Lonesome Dove — master-planned communities in Haslet — will remain zoned for Eaton rather than move to Davis. The change will involve roughly 231 students.

Parents from those neighborhoods raised concerns during an Oct. 20 community meeting at Eaton, expressing worries about transportation, existing school relationships and the differences in transfer options available to students. 

Bridgeview resident Celia Weithorn said that she lived about 3 miles from Eaton, taking her roughly 10 minutes to get to and from the campus. Traveling to Davis High would take at least half an hour, she said. 

The original proposal would have split the Hughes Elementary School attendance zone, sending students north of State Highway 170 to Byron Nelson High School and those south of the highway to Davis High School. Under the approved boundaries, the entire Hughes Elementary attendance zone will feed into Davis. 

A district survey of families in that area found most favored Davis over Byron Nelson because of the new campus’s proximity, Stewart said. Davis is closer to their homes, making it easier for parents to transport students to and from before- or after-school activities when district buses aren’t available. 

Changes are meant to balance enrollment across the district’s four comprehensive high schools. Each is designed to serve 3,200 students. Northwest High, though, currently enrolls about 3,400 students.

Even with the approval of the new boundaries, questions about which students will stay at their current high schools are unresolved. Those closest to graduation will have the option of staying at their current schools, even if they are on the transfer list. They would have to provide their own transportation and could lose the option to stay if they have behavior problems or poor attendance.

Students who remain zoned for Davis under the approved boundaries will generally be required to attend the new school as incoming freshmen or sophomores next year. Exceptions arise for students enrolled in specialized programs, staff transfers and those with a sibling entering their junior year who will stay at their current campus. 

Armando Garcia, whose children attend Byron Nelson High, asked if trustees could extend the exception to students with siblings entering their senior years.

His oldest child would be able to drive his younger sibling to school, Garcia said. A change in the exception could eliminate the need for different transportation arrangements for the two of them.

“My intent is to keep them together,” Garcia said.

While trustees discussed the request, they did not change the exception during the Nov. 9 meeting. Officials instead agreed to examine how many families would be impacted and will return with additional information before trustees consider any more revisions to the transfer rules.

Foust, the superintendent, asked trustees to separate that discussion from the boundary vote since officials have to start planning staffing and enrollment for the upcoming school year.

Northwest ISD also will not allow students to transfer freely between high schools next school year, Foust said. The district is temporarily closing limited open enrollment to help balance enrollment as Davis opens, though specialized programs and certain hardship-related transfer opportunities will remain in place.

But several trustees wondered how long the changes could remain in place.

The original proposal was intended to provide four years of stability. But keeping more students at Northwest High, combined with continued residential development in the area, could force officials to redraw the campus’s boundaries sooner than expected. Future adjustments could also affect Davis or Eaton, depending on where students are reassigned.

Residential development continuing across the sprawling district is simply one of the challenges that growth brings, he said.

Foust expressed gratitude for families’ participation in the process. He acknowledged that changes benefiting one community can create concerns for another. 

“It is impossible to make everyone happy,” Foust said. “As soon as you make someone happy and you change, you make someone mad.”

To Hill’s family and others like hers, the vote represented an opportunity to preserve school ties their children have spent years developing.

“We’ve shown up for this district for years,” Hill told trustees before the vote. “And tonight I’m asking you to show up for our children.”
