Weekend Event Planner

These are the 14 best things to do in Fort Worth this weekend

Alex Bentley
Nov 19, 2026 | 6:00 am

The weekend before Thanksgiving brings lots of holiday events in and around Fort Worth, including an ice skating rink, a theater production, a large market, a light display, a concert, fun in a theme park, and a parade. Other choices include a tribute to a Rat Packer, screenings of an acclaimed new film, a symphony concert, three country concerts, and a women's basketball showcase.

Below are the best ways to spend your free time this weekend. If you want more options, check out the calendar for an even longer list of the city's best events.

Thursday, November 19

Stockyards Rodeo Rink
Ice skating returns to the Fort Worth Stockyards National Historic District with daily skating sessions. The covered Stockyards Rodeo Rink will be open rain or shine, with all sessions running 90 minutes. Amenities include full bar and grill, warm beverages, fire pits, and picnic seating. Holiday treats include Whataburger Tuesdays where everyone who skates gets a coupon gift for a free Whataburger. Guests can also enjoy daily Herd Cattle Drives, Cowboy Santa, Holiday Workshop, and more. The rink will be open through January 3, 2027.

Casa Mañana presents The Music of Sinatra
The Music of Sinatra will be an elegant evening celebrating the life and legacy of one of the greatest entertainers of all time, Frank Sinatra. Guests will hear the stars of The Reid Cabaret Theatre bring the catalog of “Ol’ Blue Eyes” to life, plus hits from Sinatra’s daughter Nancy, including the iconic chart-topper “These Boots Are Made For Walkin.’" The production, featuring passed appetizers, a champagne toast, and black-tie requested attire, will have four performances through Sunday at Casa Mañana.

Circle Theatre presents Scrooge in Rouge
Scrooge in Rouge is a fast-paced musical comedy filled with outrageous characters, theatrical mishaps, and a holiday story that refuses to behave. When most of an acting company is unexpectedly out of commission, three remaining performers are left to carry on A Christmas Carol … no matter how unpredictable it becomes. The production runs through December 12 at Circle Theatre.

Friday, November 20

Texas Christkindl Market
The Texas Christkindl Market is a traditional German-style market bringing Old World charm and holiday magic to the heart of Texas. Visitors will find handcrafted gifts, unique collectibles, and exclusive Käthe Wohlfahrt merchandise from the Rothenburg ob der Tauber German Christmas village. The market features a variety of vendors, seasonal food and beverages, live entertainment and children’s activities, including photos with Santa. The event, which takes place in the North Plaza outside of Globe Life Field, runs through December 23.

Magnolia at the Modern: The Only Living Pickpocket in New York
Harry Lehman (John Turturro) is an aging pickpocket who plies his trade with ever-diminishing returns in a drastically changed New York. Wallets stuffed with cash have been replaced by easily canceled credit cards, and Harry struggles to make ends meet as he cares for his disabled wife, Rosie. When he unwittingly purloins and fences a valuable USB stick, Harry finds himself in a race against time to return the loot or face the rage of a vengeful crime family. The film will screen seven times through Sunday at the Modern Art Museum of Fort Worth.

Fort Worth Botanic Garden presents Christmas in the Garden
At Fort Worth Botanic Garden's Christmas in the Garden, visitors can enjoy a self-guided one-mile trail glowing with over one million lights, new installations like Neon Gallopers, Nenufars, Snowflakes, and returning seasonal favorites like the Winter Cathedral, Texas Flag, and Bluebonnets displays. Guests can also visit Santa's Village, which features music, seasonal bites, and cheerful drinks. The event takes place on select dates through January 3, 2027.

Fort Worth Symphony Orchestra presents "Copland, Dvořák, and Rachmaninoff"
Aaron Copland was the quintessential American composer in the 20th century. The Fort Worth Symphony Orchestra and conductor Tomáš Netopil will perform Copland's third and final symphony, a fusion of his famous balletic style from works like Appalachian Spring and Rodeo with the European symphonic form. The program will also include Rachmaninoff’s titanic Piano Concerto No. 2, featuring pianist Eric Lu, and Dvořák’s bright and festive Carnival Overture. There will be three performances through Sunday at Bass Performance Hall.

Justin Moore in concert
Country singer Justin Moore has been a big success ever since his debut album in 2009. That No. 3 album on the Billboard Country charts was followed by three straight No. 1 albums and another one that went to No. 2. Moore has now released eight albums in his career, most recently This is My Dirt in 2024. He'll play at Billy Bob's Texas.

Saturday, November 21

Fort Worth Opera presents Harmony for the Holidays
Fort Worth Opera will kick off the holiday season with Harmony for the Holidays, featuring holiday classics, seasonal favorites, and musical selections inspired by the singers' own cultural traditions. Audiences can expect familiar favorites, including “It's the Most Wonderful Time of the Year,” “Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer,” “Carol of the Bells,” “Jingle Bell Rock,” and “The Twelve Days of Christmas." The concert takes place at The Woman's Club of Fort Worth.

Hoops on the Square
Hoops on the Square is a fan fest that kicks off the inaugural Hoops with Heart women’s college basketball series, which takes place on Sunday at Ariat Arena. Fans can meet players from Texas, TCU, Ohio State, and Utah State, plus enjoy live music, giveaways, food trucks, and more at Sundance Square. Hoops with Heart will also feature a game between Texas and TCU on December 6.

Ty Myers in concert
Breakout country star Ty Myers, who turned the "legal" age of 18 in 2025, comes to Fort Worth as the final stop on his The Legal Tour. The arena tour is a step up for the Austin native, who was playing smaller venues like Billy Bob's Texas just last year. He's apparently got a lot to tell the world, as his new album, Two Braids & The Truth (a Willie Nelson tribute album), is his third album in under two years. He'll play at Ariat Arena.

Wade Bowen in concert
Texas country/Red Dirt singer Wade Bowen has slowly but surely been making his way up in the industry, scoring three top 10 albums in recent years. He's collaborated with Randy Rogers on three separate albums, including 2020's Hold My Beer, Vol. 2, while releasing 10 albums on his own, including the new The Version Of Me You Get. He'll play at Billy Bob's Texas.

Sunday, November 22

Six Flags Over Texas presents Holiday in the Park
Six Flags Over Texas in Arlington will transform once again into a winter wonderland for the annual Holiday in the Park. Visitors will be able to enjoy lights, immersive holiday magic, festive live shows, select Six Flags Over Texas rides & attractions, specialty holiday food & drink, and appearances by Santa. There will be a variety of new offerings for 2026, including multiple new music performances. The event takes place on select dates through December 30.

GM Financial Parade of Lights
The GM Financial Parade of Lights brightens up the streets of Downtown Fort Worth, marking the City of Fort Worth’s official kickoff to the holiday season. The 1.59-mile route features more than 100 professionally designed illuminated floats, marching bands, decorated antique cars, horse-drawn carriages and carolers, culminating with the arrival of Santa and Mrs. Claus on their specially illuminated float. For tips on parking, street closures, and more, read this primer.

Photo courtesy of Parade of Lights

The annual GM Financial Parade of Lights will take place in downtown Fort Worth on November 22.

Movie Review

Hunger Games prequel Sunrise on the Reaping gets trapped in nostalgia

Alex Bentley
Nov 18, 2026 | 4:00 pm
Photo courtesy of Lionsgate
Joseph Zada in The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping.

The Hunger Games was at the forefront of the young adult dystopian craze of the 2000s and 2010s, with the book trilogy by Suzanne Collins being adapted into four blockbuster films that collectively made almost $3 billion. With that kind of success, it’s no wonder that Collins and Lionsgate have tried to milk more out of the franchise in the form of prequels, first with The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes and now with Sunrise on the Reaping.

Even more so than Ballad…, which put future president Coriolanus Snow as the protagonist, Sunrise… leans heavily on familiar characters from the original trilogy, starting with Haymitch Abernathy (Joseph Zada). A young man counted on by many in District 12, his desire to help results in him inadvertently being chosen as one of four tributes for the 50th annual Hunger Games, aka the second Quarter Quell.

Once in Panem, Haymitch teams up with fellow tributes like Wyatt (Ben Wang) and Maysilee (McKenna Grace), gets trained by former winners like Mags (Lili Taylor) and Wiress (Maya Hawke), conspires with Beetee (Kelvin Harrison, Jr.) and Plutarch Heavensbee (Jesse Plemons), and tries not to run afoul of Drusilla Sickle (Glenn Close), Caesar Flickerman (Kieran Culkin), and especially President Snow (Ralph Fiennes).

Directed by Francis Lawrence and written by Billy Ray and Michael Lesslie, the film starts off well, establishing Haymitch’s life in District 12 with his mother, brother, and girlfriend, Lenore (Whitney Peak). But after setting up the stakes, the filmmakers struggle to maintain any kind of momentum. The biggest problem is that they pay too much attention to connections from the first film and too little to characters who aren’t as well-known, never giving this story an identity of its own.

Many of the people playing younger versions of characters from previous films feel like they’re doing cosplay, especially Caesar Flickerman and Effie Trinket (Elle Fanning). There’s a difference between reminding audiences of people they know and just playing the hits, and few of the actors get a chance to make their particular role their own. The exceptions are Plemons, who only gives a hint of Philip Seymour Hoffman in his performance, and Fiennes, who plays Snow completely differently from Donald Sutherland.

But, most importantly, there’s no juice to the story as a whole. The bloodless nature of PG-13 movies stands out here, as even brutal deaths result in very little damage. The film is full of awful dialogue that often repeats words to try to drive the point home. And there are few people for the audience to root against, as Snow is relatively muted and most of the tributes from other districts are nothing more than stereotypes with no soul to any of them.

Australian actor Zada is a new face for American audiences, and while he has a handful of good moments, he goes much too hard in the key moments of the film, resulting in a performance that is not believable. Former child actor Grace continues to try the next big thing, but doesn’t prove her worth in this role. Plemons and Fiennes come off the best among actors embodying fan-favorite characters, with most of the others feeling more like stunt casting.

The retread nature of The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping is driven home by a final scene featuring three well-known actors (whose appearance is no secret at all since they’re front-and-center in the promotion of the film). The filmmakers and Collins have fallen into the trap of Star Wars, serving up nostalgia at the cost of doing anything original.
