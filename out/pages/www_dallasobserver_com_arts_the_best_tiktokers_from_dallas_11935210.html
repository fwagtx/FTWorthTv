Dallas TikTokers We’ll Miss if the App Is Banned

TikTok is still planning to shut down this Sunday. Here are the TikTokers who we loved the most.
By Ella Coppola and Samantha ThornfeltJanuary 17, 2025
TikTok restaurant influencer Keith Lee is now a neighbor, and we'll miss his TikTok account.

TikTok has taken over the world. Gen Z loves it for its viral challenges, lip syncs, musical dances and as a platform for expression and activism. And as much as Gen Z loves it, the Supreme Court hates it.

President-elect Donald Trump signed an executive order in 2020 that declared the Chinese-owned app a national security threat, and he promised to shut it down. But, much like the infamous wall with Mexico, Trump put a hold on his plans. While he is now considering a new executive order to “save” the app, the Supreme Court is currently still upholding its Jan. 19 TikTok ban set into motion by a law signed by President Biden last year.

After hearing oral arguments about the law, the Supreme Court appeared inclined to uphold it, causing TikTok users everywhere to begin their period of mourning.

For years, a number of Dallas TikTokers have dedicated themselves to giving us the best Dallas content. Before TikTok is (maybe) gone for good, we pay tribute to our favorite Dallasites who have graced the app’s algorithm with their videos.

@ladymisskay_

Kay Poyner is one of the coolest, funniest and most unapologetically opinionated content creators out there right now. Poyner has bestowed her authentic “T girl wisdom” across TikTok since 2019, sharing personal takes and some additional shit-talking along the way. In addition to espousing insightful opinions on topics such as Gaza and trans rights, Poyner will also leave you in stitches with her ramblings on stalking vintage furniture and the dangers of cringey millennial Swifties.

Some TikTok lifestyle influencers tend to share days full of adventure, but Connor Hubbard prefers to portray the experiences of the everyday man. @keith_lee125

Keith Lee has been known as one of TikTok’s finest food reviewers long before his move to North Texas. But as of last fall, we’ve been able to proudly call him one of Dallas’ own. Lee, a former MMA fighter turned viral food critic, has gained nearly 17 million followers on the app for getting, trying and honestly rating local restaurants’ cuisine from 1 to 10. Lee has reviewed a number of DFW restaurants since his big move from Las Vegas, including Wicked Butcher and Soiree Coffee Bar.

@the_heeltor

Sam Hamway, Dallas’ beloved heel-wearing realtor, has been a bright spot in a city full of rent struggles. Hamway began going viral last year for posting lighthearted videos touring low-budget rentals across the Dallas area. While she makes sure to point out the pros of each lot – roomy living spaces, in-unit appliances and affordable pricing – her honest reviews don’t gloss over the less appealing aspects of low-budget spaces, like missing doorknobs and the occasional dead roach. Hamway is on a mission to help Dallasites ease the challenges of pricey city living through affordable rentables and a few good laughs.

@naraazizasmith

Model-turned-influencer Nara Smith and her family left Dallas for the East Coast last fall, but we’ll still partially claim her for the number of viral moments that took place in her former Big D home. Recognized for her soothing voiceovers of from-scratch meals she creates in head-to-toe designer looks, Smith quickly became one of TikTok’s most popular momfluencers for “doing too much.” Love her or hate her, Smith made her mark on the soon-to-be-defunct-app by amassing over 11.4 million followers, which recently helped land her a spot at No. 4 on Rolling Stone‘s 2024 most influential internet creators list.
