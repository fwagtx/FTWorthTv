Anthropic is a public company. What you need to know:

  • Shares of the AI powerhouse officially began trading on the Nasdaq at around $111.50 per share. After about 90 minutes of trading, the share price was hovering around $95.

IPO is 'exactly what the markets need to revive' optimism, analyst says

The reviews are starting to trickle in, and there’s one thing most analysts agree on: Anthropic's IPO underscores the massive demand for AI.

"Anthropic’s IPO is exactly what the markets need to revive overall sentiment, which has been depressed with all of the negativity in the AI complex over the past several weeks," wrote Rebecca Walser, chief investment officer at Florida-based Walser Wealth Management.

Over the past year, the AI boom has come under pressure as investors debate a bubble-bursting situation within the AI trade, pressures surrounding circular financing and negative public sentiment around data centers.

Most recently, a shocking leadership scandal at chipmaker Nvidia only added to those concerns. Jensen Huang, the company's former CEO, will testify on Capitol Hill next month. He is accused of having an affair with an alleged Chinese spy.

"It’s possible that Anthropic could see volatility and downside moves during the Jensen Huang Senate testimony after Thanksgiving, because that is likely to reignite AI fears," Walser wrote. "But if that happens, it could be a good entry point for investors who want to buy Anthropic."

Overall, Anthropic's debut represents a critical test of investor confidence: "This IPO is the show me moment for the AI story," she wrote.

Anthropic's IPO is a bellwether for future AI company debuts

Other AI companies hoping to go public — OpenAI, in particular — are squarely focused today on how much demand is generated by Anthropic's IPO.

"It’s going to help OpenAI become public," says Eddie Best, co-chair of the capital markets practice at the law firm Willkie Farr & Gallagher. If Anthropic is successful, "this will go a long way to show confidence in the broader AI market" and "help anybody in the AI ecosystem."

OpenAI, the parent company of ChatGPT, initially planned to make its market debut this year but reversed course in September, when CEO Sam Altman said going public in 2026 would be “ill-advised.”

But his tone shifted a bit yesterday at the New York Times' DealBook Summit. "We plan to be public at some point," Altman said.

It's been a rough year for tech stocks, which experienced massive gains before investors pumped the brakes in the wake of new questions about AI, such as when an AI bubble might pop and whether the technology could wipe out humanity. The leadership upheaval at Nvidia has only added to this pressure.

Everyday Americans also seem to be pushing back on AI, too: NBC News polling shows public perception of AI is increasingly negative, and was a factor in how Americans voted in the midterm elections.

"These recent months have been a newly challenging period," Altman said. "And there will be newly challenging periods ahead of us for a long time."

If all goes well for Anthropic today, though, it could help soften the blow.

Daniela Amodei on data centers: We 'need to listen to what it is those communities want '

Daniela Amodei says Anthropic needs to listen to what communities want amid the data center build-out.

"One of the things that I think is really important to Anthropic is for it to feel to people like this technology is not happening to them," Amodei says. "Building data centers is something where we also need to listen to what it is that those communities want and help to bring forward that future as well."

She says that includes making sure consumers aren't paying any cost increases.

"We’ve already committed to matching whatever price differential might happen from an electricity grid perspective, we pay for," she says. "I think that’s just one example, but I think it’s really important to us that we also go into these communities thoughtfully and that we engage with them in a way that makes us feel like it’s good for everybody."

Average household electricity costs are expected to be $1,215 this winter, up 6% from last winter, according to the latest data from the National Energy Assistance Directors Association. Economists attribute this in part to data centers, which require huge amounts of power to operate, and have deep pockets to pay higher prices for it.

Anthropic employees will hit the jackpot

Anthropic's payroll has ballooned in the past five years from a few dozen staffers to more than 5,000 employees. Most employees stand to gain millions of dollars — perhaps even tens of millions of dollars — from today's IPO.

The company has long prized teamwork and a collaborative culture — its technical employees are all termed "members of technical staff" instead of being labeled with specific job functions — but employees that joined earlier could be worth hundreds of millions of dollars more than their colleagues by the end of the day.

According to some estimates from Hill.com, a market for private-company shares, around 2,800 Anthropic employees’ shares will be worth at least $10 million at expected prices. Around 600 staffers will control shares worth more than $100 million, according to those estimates.

However, most of the employees' net worth exists on paper only: Employees are only allowed to cash out up to 10% of their equity in the company on the stock market. This stance is progressive by some metrics — many companies prohibit employees from trading any of their stock for up to six months after the company goes public.

Staffers will be able to trade "up to an additional 35%" of the equity held by employees after the fiscal year's earnings are reported, which will occur before the end of March 2027.

These state and local pension funds that will see immediate Anthropic gains

'Gray market' trading indicates Anthropic's stock could rise as much as 27% after opening

Trades on "gray markets" that allow users to trade derivative products outside of normal trading hours indicate that Anthropic shares are likely to climb as much as 27% higher than their IPO price of $83 when the stock begins trading later today.

Hong Kong-based online brokerage IG International is showing derivatives trades implying a roughly 27% pop, while crypto derivatives on the Hyperliquid blockchain show a similar figure for Anthropic's IPO pricing.

These offshore exchanges do not trade on an underlying product and are more thinly traded than regulated exchanges, but are nonetheless indicative that Anthropic's IPO is unlikely to disappoint investors.
