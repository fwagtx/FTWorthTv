Anthropic begins trading on Nasdaq in record-breaking IPO

The Claude AI maker's stock market debut follows SpaceX's June IPO as investor demand surges

Wall Street braces for Anthropic debut as AI giant eyes record stock offering

FOX Business Madison Alworth reports on Anthropics anticipated blockbuster IPO, soaring investor demand and a potential $1.4 trillion valuation as Wall Street weighs surging revenue growth against escalating AI costs.

Anthropic made its stock market debut on Thursday in a record-setting IPO amid strong investor demand for the company behind the Claude AI tool.

The company began trading at $111.50 a share on the Nasdaq under the ticker symbol "ANT."

Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 per share on Wednesday, above the initial range of $70 to $78, and is offering 980 million shares for about $81.3 billion. The company also raised $26 billion through a private placement.

The company's fully diluted valuation sits at around $1.5 trillion.

The blockbuster IPO follows SpaceX's debut in June, which raised $75 billion in its IPO at a valuation of about $1.77 trillion.

ANTHROPIC FILES SEC PAPERWORK AS AI GIANT PREPARES FOR MAJOR IPO

Anthropic's highly-anticipated IPO follows the record-setting debut of SpaceX in June. (Samyukta Lakshmi/Bloomberg via Getty Images)

While traditional IPOs tend to have lock-up periods that restrict the ability of insiders to sell shares for about six months, Anthropic is allowing some of its existing shareholders to sell a portion of their holdings when trading begins.

The company's SEC filing indicated that as many as 315 million shares held by non-founder executives, directors, and current and former employees and consultants could become eligible for sale on the stock's first trading day. These shareholders are generally allowed to sell up to 10% of their locked-up holdings immediately.

Its other shareholders face staggered lock-up periods for their holdings, with restrictions of up to one year applicable to founders and certain large shareholders.

TRUMP HOSTING ANTHROPIC CEO DARIO AMODEI FOR PRIVATE WHITE HOUSE DINNER

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has said AI developers need to "pace the frontier" of the emerging tech to mitigate risks to humanity. (Finn Gomez/Getty Images)

The company said that it plans to allocate up to 20% of its IPO shares to retail investors in the U.S. through platforms like Fidelity, Robinhood, Charles Schwab, SoFi and E*TRADE – though the final allocation will depend on demand.

Founders will retain 50.1% of voting power on general shareholder matters through a special class of shares, while the company's Long-Term Benefit Trust will also generally control the selection of a majority of the company's board.

Anthropic was founded in 2021 and is led by co-founder and CEO Dario Amodei and co-founder and president Daniela Amodei, who are siblings. The company rose to prominence with its Claude AI tool, which emerged as a rival to OpenAI's ChatGPT.

The Nasdaq MarketSite on the day Anthropic holds its initial public offering in New York City, on Nov. 19, 2026. (Mike Segar/Reuters / Reuters)

The company's regulatory filings ahead of its IPO revealed it brought in $33.2 billion in revenue in the first nine months of 2026, which represented an increase of 1,206% from the same period last year.

Anthropic reported a net loss of $104 billion that was largely driven by noncash accounting charges, though it generated $6.6 billion in positive operating cash flow.

The company disclosed about $900.9 billion in spending commitments, most of which are related to computing power and infrastructure, that extend through 2045. Of that total, about $70.7 billion is due within 12 months from Sept. 30, 2026.

The company's regulatory filing also disclosed a risk from the potential "end of humanity" due to their product and AI.

Dario Amodei has called for labs developing cutting-edge AI models, including Anthropic, to "pace the frontier" of models' capabilities to mitigate risks to society from the tool advancing to a degree that developers are unable to control it.

FOX Business' Madison Alworth contributed to this report.
